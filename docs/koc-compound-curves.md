# Koc compound curves: satellite-survey railway alignment, mechanised

**Source.** W. Koc, *"Design of compound curves adapted to the satellite
measurements"*, The Archives of Transport 34(2), 2015, 37–49
(doi:10.5604/08669546.1169211). Mechanised in
`theories/CompoundCurveKoc.v` (all `Qed`, three-axiom, sqrt-free).

## The real-world problem

Polish railways (PKP PLK, with Gdańsk University of Technology and the
Polish Naval Academy) survey existing track by **mobile GNSS**: antennas on
a rail trailer stream the as-built axis as coordinates in the national grid
("system 2000", EPSG:2176–2180), against the ASG-EUPOS active network. The
measured shape drives modernization design.

On degraded lines the surveyed track is often so deformed that the original
design straights (the "main directions") **cannot be recovered**. The
classic symmetric pattern — transition curve, one circular arc, transition
curve — is then unusable, and EN 13803-1's **compound curve** is the only
option: two circular arcs of *different* radii `R1`, `R2` joined by three
transition curves TC1–TC3, connecting the two surveyed directions.

Koc's method expresses the *entire* five-element system as closed-form
equations in a local frame. Design variants are generated by substituting
numbers (radii, transition lengths) into the formulas — not by redrawing
CAD geometry — and the chosen variant is transformed back to the grid for
stakeout. This is exactly the computational pattern a GIS/geometry backend
(NTS in rail asset systems, LandXML ingestion, track-axis regression)
executes, and every step has a concrete floating-point failure mode.

## Proof engineering notes

- **Sqrt-free normalisation.** The paper divides by `√(1+s²)` everywhere.
  The lemmas instead carry `u > 0, u² = 1+s²` as hypotheses — the same move
  as `CurveJoinClassify.unit_eq_iff_cross_dot` avoiding `/r` — so every
  statement is rational in its inputs and closes by `field`/`ring`, and the
  3-4-5 example needs no `sqrt` at all.
- **Robust trig round-trips.** `nra` cannot find witnesses for the
  Pythagorean-identity rewrites; the proofs use explicit
  `replace … by ring; rewrite (sin²+cos²=1)` steps instead.
- **Axiom footprint.** All headliners: exactly the classical-reals trio
  (`sig_not_dec`, `sig_forall_dec`, `functional_extensionality_dep`);
  no `Classical_Prop.classic`.

## Relation to the existing corpus

The clothoid lane (`ClothoidBufferBridge.v` etc.) models curvature in
normalized τ for *offset safety*; this file adds the **alignment-design**
end conditions in arc length (θ′ = k, θ(L) = L/(2R)) that the buffer lane
never needed. The tangency lemmas are the R-side complement of the arc
machinery (`ArcChordApprox`, `ArcDistance`): centre-from-tangent-point and
point-from-centre-with-prescribed-slope, both direction of the same normal
algebra. Natural next rungs: the compound-curve *assembly* invariant
(tangent conformity at all four joints K1–K3, O2 as one theorem chain), and
the symmetric main-direction intersection (eqs (37)–(41)) that places the
local frame in the grid.

## Part 2 — pinning the frame to the grid (`CompoundCurveKocFrame.v`)

Koc §7–§8 (eqs (37)–(41)), the step that turns an in-frame design into
stakeout coordinates. A wrong vertex or a wrong walk direction shifts the
*entire* alignment along the old track axis — internally perfect geometry,
globally misplaced, caught only when the setting-out crew finds the curve
starting metres from where the survey said.

| Koc eq. | Lemma | Content |
